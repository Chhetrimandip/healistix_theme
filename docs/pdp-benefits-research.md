## Evidence and limits

Product quantities come from the existing Banana label transcription in snippets/hx-label-banana.liquid: serving 2 tbsp (28g), iron 15% DV, folate 15% DV, B6 10% DV. These are manufacturer label values, not an independent laboratory verification. Nutrient function is separate from evidence that taking this gel improves health outcomes.

- NIH iron: https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-Consumer/ — hemoglobin carries oxygen; myoglobin supplies muscles.
- NIH folate: https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Folate-Consumer/ — DNA production and cell division.
- NIH B6: https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB6-Consumer/ — metabolism and immune function.
- Cleveland Clinic: https://health.clevelandclinic.org/sea-moss-benefits — potential benefits, but sea moss itself is insufficiently studied and composition varies.
- NIH iodine: https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iodine-HealthProfessional/ — thyroid physiology does not establish iodine content or efficacy of this jar.

Did not add claims about detox, weight loss, curing conditions, boosted energy, clearer skin, reduced bloating, 92 minerals or clinically proven efficacy. Banana label reports 0g fiber and vitamin C 0% DV; those do not support the previously suggested fiber/vitamin C benefit story. Product iodine amount is not established here.

## Brand positioning

Recommended proposition: “Wildcrafted St Lucia sea moss. Real banana purée. Ready for your everyday.” This is a combination of product features, not a claim that competitors lack them. The Banana label supports the four listed ingredients and origin. Ready-to-use convenience follows from the gel format and label directions.

The live homepage currently says all jars contain a six-ingredient Trinity blend; that conflicts with the four-ingredient Banana label. Do not reuse the blanket Trinity claim on this PDP. Broader sourcing certifications, batch testing, clinical efficacy, nutrient assays and exclusivity need their own evidence before becoming USPs.
