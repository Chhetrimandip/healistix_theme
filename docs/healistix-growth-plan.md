# Healistix: first 90 days of growth

Draft dated 2026-09-08. User budget: approximately $1,000/month in Meta ad spend, with a separate modest UGC budget. Working assumptions: US customers and Shopify as the paid-traffic destination. These assumptions, margin, fulfillment capacity, and current Amazon sales need confirmation. No campaign, creator booking, or spend has been authorized by this document.

## First objective

Find one repeatable combination of flavor, creative, offer, and product page that generates orders at an affordable acquisition cost. Start with the five-flavor asset pilot; do not split a small ad budget across every product and multiple advertising platforms.

The inspected Shopify Sea Moss product showed one unit sold in its past-90-days panel. This is a limited product-level observation, not a statement about brand-wide revenue, Amazon performance, or total demand. Establish a baseline before forecasting.

## Weeks 1–2: make the destination ready

- Finish the five flavor galleries and the explicit flavor-profile content structure. Reconcile Pineapple's separate product and the absent Watermelon variant without losing existing product URLs or guessing inventory.
- Confirm stock, shipping area, delivery expectations, shelf life/storage instructions, and approved ingredient/nutrition information. Replace Trinity copy where it does not describe the actual flavor. Do not send paid traffic into unverified product promises.
- Replace invented review placeholders with authentic sourced content. Initially link to Amazon without claiming a rating we have not verified. See the testimonial workflow below.
- Check product selection, mobile gallery, add-to-cart, checkout handoff, shipping charges, and discount application. Configure and verify consent-aware Meta purchase tracking; use order records to check reported purchases and avoid double counting.
- Keep the provisional 30% signup offer only if the economics work. Free shipping remains undecided. A playful reveal can disclose a fixed offer; do not imply a random prize or invented deadline if there is none.
- Draft welcome, abandoned-checkout, and post-purchase care/review emails. Configure them through the chosen email platform only after offer, consent and timing are settled. No messages sent as part of this plan.

## Know the affordable acquisition cost

Calculate contribution before ads per order:

`net product revenue + shipping collected − product cost − packaging − fulfillment − shipping paid − payment fees − expected refund/spoilage cost`

Use this amount as the first-order break-even acquisition cost. Set the operating target lower to leave a profit/cash buffer. Do not justify losses using repeat orders until repeat-order behavior has actually been measured.

Illustration using the currently displayed $24.99 price: a 30% discount leaves about $17.49 in product revenue before all costs. This is not a margin estimate. Fill in actual costs before deciding that an offer or a $20 acquisition cost is viable.

## Weeks 2–4: small creative test

Proposed starting setup, subject to account/tracking readiness:

- One Meta sales campaign, one prospecting ad set, one Shopify destination, and three or four distinct ads. Approximately $33/day fits a $1,000 30-day month; monitor actual month-to-date spend against the cap.
- Avoid dividing this budget into many interests, flavors and retargeting pools. Add a separate retargeting allocation later only if the audience and delivery justify it.
- Test creative concepts rather than tiny cosmetic variations: first taste/texture, a simple serving demonstration, and an authentic maker/process story supported by real footage.
- Use the same offer during the initial creative comparison. At this budget, treat results as directional; a few orders do not establish statistical certainty.
- Log spend, landing-page views, add-to-carts, checkouts, purchases, net revenue, new-customer cost, and contribution after ads. Click-through rate helps diagnose an ad; purchases and contribution decide whether to keep funding it.

Working loss-control rule: investigate an ad when it spends around 1.5–2 times the agreed target acquisition cost without an order. Check tracking and the purchase path before calling it a creative failure. This is a proposed cash-control rule, not a platform requirement or a statistically reliable test threshold.

## UGC: start with two creators

Seek quotes for two creators before commissioning a larger batch. A proposed first-batch ceiling is $300–$500 total, separate from ad spend, including product/shipping and usage rights where possible. This is a budget recommendation, not a claim about prevailing creator rates. Reduce the scope if usable quotes exceed the ceiling; do not book automatically.

Request from each creator:

- One vertical 20–35 second edit, two alternative opening hooks, and a small set of raw product/serving clips.
- Real product use and honest taste/texture reactions. No scripted medical outcomes or instruction to pretend to be a long-term customer.
- Clear deliverables, delivery date, one revision round, agreed organic/paid usage term, and whether creator-handle advertising or raw footage are included. Record licensing before reusing footage.
- Disclosure of payment or gifted product when an endorsement is published. Never make payment or gifts conditional on a positive review.

Use founder-shot material between creator batches: spoon texture, jar/label closeups, serving ideas, packaging, and the real fulfillment process. Do not manufacture a customer testimonial from AI video or an actor reading a review.

## Month 2: refine what works

- Keep the creative concepts that drive affordable orders; replace weak concepts rather than redesigning the entire funnel each week.
- Test one major PDP/offer change at a time. Consider a bundle only after shipping, margin, cold-storage needs, and demand make it sensible.
- Build a small library of honest customer feedback, serving demonstrations, and answers to actual pre-purchase questions.
- Review cancellation/refund reasons and shipping issues alongside ads. Pause acquisition if fulfillment cannot support it.
- Keep total Meta spending within the agreed monthly cap; no automatic budget scaling.

## Month 3: retention and a scale decision

- Measure repeat purchases by customer cohort and time since first order. Time replenishment communications using actual usage/storage information and customer behavior, not an invented jar depletion period.
- Evaluate the whole period's contribution after advertising, creator costs, discounts and returns. Separate Amazon and Shopify results.
- Increase the budget only if contribution, acquisition cost and fulfillment remain acceptable over a meaningful sample. Otherwise keep the budget flat and fix the most evidenced bottleneck.

## Testimonials before video exists

Text reviews are sufficient to start. For each proposed excerpt, capture the exact original text, rating, reviewer display name, date, product/flavor/size, individual source URL, and reuse-permission/licensing status. Confirm the Amazon listing actually corresponds to the product shown. A shared Amazon variation-family rating must not be presented as a specific Banana rating.

Use a plainly labeled “Amazon customer review” card with source link, flavor/date, and a faithful excerpt once reuse is cleared. Use “Verified Purchase” only when that source review actually carries the designation. Do not describe imported Amazon reviews as verified Shopify orders, invent review counts, or combine ratings from different sources without a valid calculation and clear labels.

The provided Amazon link currently redirects to sign-in in the available research browser. No review excerpts, rating totals, or product identity were verified. The development PDP therefore links to the supplied review page and omits the prior fictional reviewer cards and unverified 460+ rating claim.

When video arrives, add it beside the text reviews using the same source and disclosure discipline. Review content containing health claims before advertising it; a customer's claim is not evidence of a product benefit.

## Weekly 30-minute review

1. What was spent, and what net sales/contribution resulted?
2. Is the problem before the click, on the PDP, in checkout, or after delivery?
3. What single change will we test next week, and what result would justify keeping it?
4. Are inventory, cash, and creator commitments within the agreed limits?
