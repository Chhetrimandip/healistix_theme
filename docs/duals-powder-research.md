A listing's marketing claims are not clinical substantiation for a finished Healistix blend. Current public JSON showed all seven products available at retrieval; this does not reserve supply or establish future inventory.

The shortlist informs the ingredient library; it is not a recommended combination or recipe. Topical preparations, edible powders and sea-moss additions are not interchangeable. The client must choose intended use and have the final formula, preparation, amounts, contraindications, allergens and shelf life reviewed. Several source pages carry pregnancy/nursing restrictions; the funnel must not override those through a quiz answer.

## Copy direction

Use hair care and a personal routine as the campaign topic. Describe powder identity, taste, intended preparation once approved, and how the customer's preferences shape the experience. Do not claim to diagnose hair-loss causes, stop shedding, regrow hair, or deliver a guaranteed result. Source citations are supplied for review, not proof of clinical effectiveness.

[FTC health-product guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance) explains that express and implied health claims need appropriate substantiation. A disclaimer does not make an unsupported promise acceptable.
